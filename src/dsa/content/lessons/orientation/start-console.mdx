## Why it matters

You're about to learn two things at once: the Swift language and data structures and algorithms (DSA). That's a long road, roughly 250 hours of lessons from start to finish. This console is built so you never have to wonder what to do next. Ten minutes here saves hours of drifting later.

## What DSA actually is

A **data structure** is a way of organising data so that some operations become fast. An **algorithm** is a precise, step-by-step recipe for solving a problem. They come as a pair: choosing a good structure is often what makes a fast algorithm possible.

<Callout kind="analogy">
Think of a library. Throwing every book on the floor is a data structure too, just a terrible one: finding a book means checking them all. Shelving books alphabetically is a better structure, and "go to the right letter, then scan" is the algorithm it enables. DSA is the study of shelving systems and the searches they make possible.
</Callout>

You already use DSA every day as an iOS developer, even when it's hidden. A `Dictionary` is a hash table. `sort()` is a carefully tuned sorting algorithm. A `UINavigationController` keeps its screens on a stack. The SwiftUI view hierarchy is a tree. Learning DSA means understanding what's happening underneath, so you can choose well and build your own when the standard library doesn't have what you need.

## How the console is organised

The curriculum has **11 parts** and **75 modules**, from Swift basics to advanced algorithms. Each module is a handful of lessons, and every lesson follows the same shape:

1. **Why it matters**: where the idea shows up in real code and interviews.
2. **Concept**: explained in small steps, with an analogy and often a visualiser you can step through.
3. **Swift code**: real, runnable examples with their output.
4. **Gotchas and takeaways**: the mistakes to avoid and the points worth remembering.
5. **Quiz**: a few questions to check your understanding, with an explanation for every answer.
6. **Practice**: problems to solve in the companion Swift package.

## Your goal shapes your path

In **Settings** you choose a goal. Every lesson is tagged with one or more tracks: **Core**, **Interview**, **iOS**, **Competitive** and **Deep dive**. Your goal decides which tracks make up your main path. Nothing is hidden: lessons outside your goal still appear on the Path page as side quests, marked with their track.

Start with **Strong foundations**. You can widen the goal at any point and your progress carries over.

## How progress works

- **Mark complete** at the end of a lesson. Its key takeaways become review cards, due tomorrow.
- **Quiz answers** are saved. A question you miss on your first try becomes a review card too.
- **Problems** are logged with how it went and when you'd like to see them again.
- **XP and your streak** are worked out from all of this, so they're always accurate.

Your progress syncs to your account, so your Mac and phone always agree. It also works offline: answer a quiz on a train and it syncs when you're back online.
