## Why it matters

Most people who give up on DSA don't lack ability. They study in ways that *feel* productive but don't stick: rereading notes, watching solutions, copying code. Learning science has clear answers about what works. Using them can easily halve the time it takes you to really know this material.

## 1. Active recall beats rereading

Rereading makes material feel familiar, and familiarity is easily mistaken for knowing. **Retrieving** information, pulling it out of your memory without looking, is what strengthens it.

That's why every lesson ends with a quiz, and why you should answer *before* scrolling back up. Struggling to remember, and even getting it wrong, makes the memory stronger.

## 2. Spaced repetition beats cramming

You forget most of a new idea within days unless you revisit it. Review it just before you'd forget, and each review makes it last longer: a day, then three, then a week, then a month.

The **Review** screen does this scheduling for you. Your job is to show up and rate honestly:

| Rating | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| **Again** | You couldn't recall it. It comes back in ten minutes. |
| **Hard** | You got it, but slowly or shakily. |
| **Good** | You recalled it with normal effort. |
| **Easy** | It was instant. It won't come back for a while. |

<Callout kind="warning">
Rating "Good" when the honest answer is "Again" only fools the schedule. The card then disappears for days, right when you needed it.
</Callout>

## 3. Struggle first, then get help

When you're stuck on a problem, that's when learning happens. Use the **25-minute rule**:

1. Give the problem a real, focused attempt of about 25 minutes. Write *something*: brute force counts.
2. Still stuck? Open **one** hint, then try again.
3. Only after the hints, read an approach. Then **close it and write the solution yourself** from memory.
4. Log it honestly. "Solved with help" schedules it to come back, so you prove you can do it alone.

## 4. Mix it up once you know the basics

Practising one pattern ten times in a row feels smooth but teaches you less than you'd think, because you always know which tool to use. Real problems don't announce their pattern. Once you've learned a few topics, **mix** your practice. The "Random from what I've learned" button on the Practice page does exactly this.

## 5. Explain it in plain words

If you can't explain an idea simply, you don't understand it yet. After a lesson, write a few sentences in the notes box as if teaching a friend. The gaps you find are exactly what to revisit.

## A healthy routine

- **Little and often**: 45 focused minutes most days beats a five-hour weekend binge.
- **Reviews first**: they're quick and keep everything you've learned alive.
- **Then something new**: a lesson, then practice on it while it's fresh.
- **Protect your streak, but not at all costs.** A missed day isn't a failure. Just come back tomorrow.
